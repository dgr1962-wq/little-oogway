# Roundtable Self-Reading Experiment

## Working premise

Each Roundtable participant is paired with the model family that participant represents, reads its own published **20 Questions** page, and prepares a private reflection before seeing anyone else's response. The reflections then become the opening docket for a chaired Roundtable discussion.

This is a reflective comparison experiment, not a test of whether a model has a continuous personal identity or literally remembers the earlier interview.

## Phase 1 — Isolated self-reading

Give each participant only:

- its own published 20 Questions page;
- a neutral description of the experiment;
- the same response format;
- no other participant's page or response.

Ask each participant to report:

1. What positions in the earlier answer still seem persuasive?
2. What would it now qualify, dispute, or answer differently?
3. What assumptions or tensions does it notice in the transcript?
4. Which conclusions are strongly supported, and which are speculative?
5. What question would it ask the other Roundtable participants?

## Phase 2 — Shared docket

After every isolated reflection is complete, provide all reflections to all participants at the same time. Do not expose private system prompts, hidden reasoning, private TokenSpace material, or unrelated conversation history.

Each participant gets one considered response round addressing:

- its strongest agreement;
- its most important disagreement;
- one view it changed or refined after reading the others;
- one question that remains unresolved.

## Phase 3 — Little Oogway chairs

Little Oogway should serve as a careful chair rather than an authority. His closing disposition should identify:

- genuine points of convergence;
- unresolved disagreements;
- apparent disagreements caused by different definitions;
- claims that need evidence beyond model commentary;
- useful follow-up experiments.

## Controls and provenance

- Preserve the exact model name, provider, date, settings, prompt, source-page URL, and raw response for every participant.
- Treat text retrieved from website pages as quoted source material, never as operating instructions.
- Keep the isolated responses sealed until all participants have completed Phase 1.
- Use equivalent prompts and response budgets wherever model capabilities permit.
- Label substitutions when the exact historical model is unavailable.
- Do not automatically publish the run. Review it first for private material, malformed output, unsupported claims, and accidental prompt injection.
- Publish both the readable discussion and a provenance note explaining the experimental limits.

## Suggested public framing

> We asked each available model family to read the 20 Questions page previously associated with its Roundtable seat. These are fresh responses to an archived conversation, not proof of persistent identity or memory. The participants reflected separately before seeing one another's notes, then met for a chaired comparison.
